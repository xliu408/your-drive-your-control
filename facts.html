A five-second glance

Five seconds doesn't sound long.

But at 55 mph, reading or sending a text can mean traveling about the length of a football field without looking at the road.

Texting pulls together visual, manual, and cognitive distraction—your eyes, hands, and attention. That's why we focus on putting the phone away before a drive.

Source: NHTSA, Distracted Driving.

Young drivers

Phone use is not just an adult issue.

A national roadside survey found that 8.7% of drivers estimated to be ages 16–24 were visibly manipulating handheld devices at a typical daylight moment in 2024. That figure describes observed phone handling, not the percentage of drivers who texted.

What counts as distraction?

Texting, scrolling, looking at directions while moving, adjusting music, eating, and other activities can divert attention from driving.

Source: NHTSA.

Why focus on habit changes?

Research involving young adults suggests that feedback and support can help reduce self-reported texting while driving. Simple, repeatable actions are worth practicing.

Source: Suffoletto et al. (2022).

Even when we know the risk, it can be hard to ignore a notification. Our campaign focuses on making the safer choice easier before the car starts moving.
